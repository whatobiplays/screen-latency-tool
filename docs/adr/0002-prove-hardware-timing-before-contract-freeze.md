# Prove critical hardware timing before freezing v1 implementation contracts

Status: Accepted (2026-10-29)

Use a hardware-first feasibility milestone before production PCB fabrication or full subsystem implementation. Prove Teensy 4.1 Full-Speed composite enumeration, measured HID-transfer completion correlation to the fixture timebase, five hardware-capture channels on a defensible common timebase, and a viable optical sensor path. These are the highest-risk constraints and failure may require alternate capture circuitry or protocol details. The rejected approach was to execute the five software/firmware/mechanical subsystem plans sequentially and freeze their detailed contracts before these risks are measured.

Consequences: keep the approved measurement meaning and acceptance targets unchanged, but treat implementation-level USB/record layouts as provisional until evidence resolves the feasibility gate. Before prototyping, consolidate the governing methodology, glossary, and minimal feasibility criteria in the Argus-style documentation hierarchy so that the prototype is testable against an agreed contract. After the feasibility gate, reconcile and freeze detailed production USB/session contracts and the downstream subsystem specifications, then implement independently verifiable vertical slices. Hardware acceptance cannot be substituted with simulation or host-only tests.

The feasibility milestone must also demonstrate a real minimal end-to-end path before full PCB commitment: the Teensy sends a measured HID press to an actual Android device running a minimal but reusable rendering implementation; the display changes state; one optical sensor observes the transition; and the raw measurement can be correlated to the measured HID completion. Separately prove all five hardware capture inputs and characterize USB completion timing with external instrumentation. Do not equate a first working optical transition with final timing qualification or with an accepted production data contract. The rejected electronics-only feasibility milestone would defer an important Android/USB/rendering integration risk.

Prototype policy (accepted 2026-10-29): solderless breadboards, breakout modules, and temporary wiring are encouraged for low-risk Teensy GPIO/control logic, LED stimulation, and functional smoke tests before board orders. Do not route USB Full-Speed D+/D- or the high-impedance photodiode-to-TIA measurement front end through solderless breadboards. Use a proper short USB breakout/probe arrangement, and a compact, locally decoupled sensor breakout, evaluation board, or prototype PCB for optical timing characterization. Breadboard-based capture routing is provisional functional evidence, not final measurement uncertainty proof. The owner may solder whatever connectors, through-hole parts, short wires, evaluation boards, or prototype surface-mount parts are needed during the prototyping stage, using appropriate soldering and inspection methods. Professionally fabricate/assemble final production sensor/carrier PCBs after the feasibility milestone; qualify the final assembled revision independently.

Purchasing sequence (accepted 2026-10-29): first buy the Teensy and inexpensive digital/optical prototyping materials; acquire the planned DHO814 or equivalent oscilloscope when externally characterizing successful USB HID transfer completion and timing uncertainty becomes the next blocking activity; order the professionally assembled production PCB revision only after the hardware feasibility gate passes. The reason for staging is to avoid committing the largest purchase and fabrication costs before low-cost functional risks are retired. Staging purchases does not authorize skipping oscilloscope-based timing characterization or claiming publication-grade readiness from functional prototype results. Maintain the agreed $1,100 overall target with discretionary flexibility to approximately $1,300.
